Training case · Product Discovery · Medium level

Sprint Discovery backlog · Jabu, YC S21

Jabu delivers groceries from manufacturers and distributors to neighbourhood stores in Namibia, Zambia and South Africa. I wrote solution hypotheses and built a one-week Discovery sprint backlog for a team of three — a PM, a UX researcher and a product designer — with Story Point estimates, dependencies and a day-by-day plan.

A study plan, not work inside Jabu. Product facts come from public sources 2022–2025; all thresholds and percentages are assumptions to test. Download the case PDF (RU) ↓

What Jabu already has

  • Ordering and payment in the app, same-day delivery (Jwallet)
  • Credit products for stores
  • Field agents who onboard and support stores
  • Customer support in WhatsApp groups
  • JabuPay and the MTN partnership in Zambia: instant payments and less cash

The Discovery job is not to reinvent these features but to understand why they do not turn the first order into a habit. So every hypothesis is about improving what already exists.

Sprint metric and opportunity tree

GMV = active stores × orders per store per month × average order value
Sprint metricShare of stores placing a 2nd order within 30 days of the 1st — M1 repeat rate
GuardrailsOverdue loan rate and delivery cost per order — so growth is not bought with bad debt or expensive delivery
Opportunity (owner’s pain)Hypotheses

O3 “Ordering in the app is harder than texting or calling”H3 ★, H5
O4 “I don’t see how it beats cash & carry”H4

Solution hypotheses

Format: “If we do X, metric Y will change by Z, because W.” ICE = Impact × Confidence × Ease (1–10); Confidence is explained by the level of evidence. Success and kill criteria are written before the research.

H1O1 · ★ICE 8·7·6 = 336

If we show an exact delivery window at checkout and warn about shortages before dispatch with a substitute option, the share of stores placing a 2nd order within 30 days will grow by 8 pp, because incomplete and late deliveries send owners back to cash & carry.

Riskiest assumption
Short and late deliveries happen often, and owners blame Jabu for them.
Metric
M1 repeat rate
Success if
The data shows a repeat-order gap of ≥ 10 pp between a perfect and a problematic 1st delivery, and ≥ 5 of 10 respondents name delivery reliability as the reason.
Kill if
The gap is < 3 pp and reliability does not come up in interviews.
Confidence
C = 7: tested on real delivery data.
H2O2 · ★ICE 9·5·6 = 270

If we offer the existing credit right in the cart and show its cost in money rather than percent, the share of orders on credit will double and these stores’ average order will grow by 15%, because stores don’t know about the credit or don’t understand its terms.

Riskiest assumption
Credit is underused because of unawareness, not fear of debt or its price.
Metric
Share of orders on credit, average order; guardrail — overdue rate
Success if
Most eligible stores don’t use credit, and ≥ 5 of 10 interviewees explain it by unawareness or not understanding the terms.
Kill if
The reason is fear of debt or the price of credit. Then it is a different hypothesis, not about the interface.
Confidence
C = 5: the reason for low usage is still unknown.
H3O3 · ★ICE 7·6·5 = 210

If a store can repeat its last order with one WhatsApp message, order frequency among active stores will grow by 15%, because owners already talk to Jabu on WhatsApp, while the app is heavy for cheap phones and expensive data.

Riskiest assumption
Store orders repeat enough for a “repeat” to be useful.
Metric
Orders per store per month
Success if
≥ 6 of 10 regularly order a similar basket, complete the repeat order in the prototype unaided, and the tech lead confirms feasibility.
Kill if
The basket differs every time, or ordering through an agent already covers this need.
Confidence
C = 6: support already runs on WhatsApp, but demand for ordering there is untested.

Tasks as Job Stories

Format: “When [situation], I want to [action], so that [outcome].” Each estimate is explained — why this number and why not the neighbouring one. Tap a task to expand it.

Product manager — 22 SP

PM-1Metric tree, OST and decision rulesH1–H3 · Mon · 3 SP
Job Story
When I, as a PM, start a one-week sprint with six hypotheses, I want to link them to the metric tree and write success and kill criteria for the top three upfront, so that on Friday the decision follows rules, not impressions.
Why
3 SP — The method is known but thresholds are uncertain: there are no baselines yet and they must be agreed with the CEO and ops. Not 2 — because of sign-offs; not 5 — no data work, these are decisions.
Done when
Metric tree and OST on one page; success and kill criteria agreed with the CEO.
Dependencies
—
PM-2Retention cohorts and delivery reliabilityH1 · Mon–Wed · 5 SP
Job Story
When I, as a PM, suspect stores leave because of incomplete and late deliveries, I want to compare repeat orders of stores with a perfect versus a problematic first delivery across three countries, so that I can confirm or kill H1 on data and give the researcher segments for recruiting.
Why
5 SP — High data uncertainty: orders, logistics and the agents’ CRM live in different systems, three currencies, and “incomplete delivery” may not be logged — we will need to agree on a proxy. Not 8: the data schema is known, no new tracking is needed.
Done when
M1 cohort table by segment; store lists for recruiting by Tuesday evening.
Dependencies
PM-1 → UXR-2
PM-3Existing credit funnelH2 · Wed · 3 SP
Job Story
When I, as a PM, know credit already exists but not why it is underused, I want to build the funnel “eligible → saw the offer → took it → repaid on time”, so that I see where stores drop off and don’t test credit in the cart blindly.
Why
3 SP — The data sits with the credit team, but a “saw the offer” event may not exist — a proxy is needed; the data is sensitive, access goes through finance. Not 5: one funnel, not a model.
Done when
Funnel by country and the overdue rate as a guardrail.
Dependencies
PM-1 → D-3
PM-4Interviews with field staffH1–H3 · Mon · 3 SP
Job Story
When I, as a PM, prepare for customer interviews, I want to talk to 5–6 field people — agents, dispatchers, the credit team and WhatsApp support — so that I collect the churn reasons and operational limits already known and don’t spend customer interviews on what the company already knows.
Why
3 SP — Calls are easy to book, but opinions contradict each other: they must be reconciled and facts separated from opinions. Not 2 — because of the synthesis; not 5 — the people are inside the company.
Done when
A list of churn reasons marked “fact / opinion”; handed to UXR-1.
Dependencies
→ UXR-1, D-5
PM-5WhatsApp ordering feasibilityH3 · Tue · 2 SP
Job Story
When I, as a PM, consider repeat ordering in WhatsApp, I want to assess WhatsApp Business API limits, message costs and the link to the cart in one call with the tech lead, so that I don’t show customers a solution that can’t be built or costs too much.
Why
2 SP — A narrow question, the tech lead has the answer, the output is one page. Not 1: message costs must be calculated for three countries.
Done when
A “can / can’t / what it costs” note for H3.
Dependencies
→ D-4
PM-6Experiment cards for sprint 2H1–H3 · Thu · 3 SP
Job Story
When by Thursday it is clear which hypotheses are alive, I want to describe an experiment for each — a delivery-window pilot on one route, a fake door for credit in the cart, a manual WhatsApp repeat order for 30 stores — with sample, duration and threshold, so that sprint 2 starts with a launch, not with planning.
Why
3 SP — Three experiments, each to be agreed with ops or engineering and sized for low traffic. The uncertainty is which hypotheses survive until Thursday.
Done when
3 experiment cards agreed with ops and the tech lead.

PM-7Synthesis, decision log and demoH1–H3 · Fri · 3 SP
Job Story

Why
3 SP — Others prepare the materials, but data and interviews may contradict each other — the conflict must be worked through honestly, not resolved by picking the convenient version.

UX researcher — 21 SP

UXR-1Research plan and interview guideH1–H3 · Mon · 3 SP
Job Story
When I, as a researcher, receive three hypotheses and churn reasons from stakeholders, I want to write a JTBD guide — how the store buys stock today, what breaks, how it pays — with past-experience questions before any prototype, so that interviews test the hypotheses rather than confirm them.
Why
3 SP — It must be adapted for three countries and interpreters; questions about money and debt are sensitive and must be phrased without leading. Not 5: a guide template exists.
Done when
Guide and screener; the guide piloted with one agent.
Dependencies
PM-1, PM-4
UXR-2Respondent recruitingH1–H3 · Mon–Tue · 5 SP
Job Story
When I, as a researcher, need to reach owners who are in the store all day and often without a stable connection, I want to recruit 12 people from three segments — regular buyers, those who left after 1–2 orders, credit users — via agents and WhatsApp, with interpreters and incentives, so that I can run 10 interviews this week with a buffer for no-shows.
Why
5 SP — The main schedule risk: churned customers are reluctant to reply, interpreters and incentives (airtime or a voucher) are needed. Not 8: agents know the owners personally, and until PM-2 segments are ready we start from the agents’ lists.
Done when
12 confirmed slots, ≥ 3 per segment, at least 2 countries.
Dependencies
PM-2
UXR-3In-depth interviewsH1–H3 · Tue–Thu · 5 SP
Job Story
When respondents are recruited, I want to run 10 interviews of 45 minutes — some in the store, watching how the owner checks stock and orders — so that I find the real reasons the first order did not become a habit.
Why
5 SP — 10 interviews with an interpreter, travel and dropped connections; the uncertainty is no-shows and translation quality. Not 8: the concept test is a separate task, and recruiting is done in UXR-2.
Done when
10 interviews; notes on the shared board within 2 hours of each.
Dependencies
UXR-1, UXR-2
UXR-4Prototype concept testH1–H3 · Thu–Fri · 3 SP
Job Story
When the designer prepares prototypes, I want to show them one by one at the end of 5–6 interviews and ask for a task — choose a delivery window, take credit in the cart, repeat an order in WhatsApp — so that I see whether owners understand the solution without explanation.
Why
3 SP — Prototypes arrive on different days, the showing order must be rotated, and without a connection they are shown on printouts (D-7).
Done when
An “understood / completed / objections” table per prototype.

D-3, D-4, D-5, D-7

Risks and what we don’t do

RiskWhat we do
Respondent no-showsRecruit 12 for 10 interviews; agents know the owners personally.
No data on incomplete deliveriesProxy: orders with a return or an amount correction after delivery.
Language and connectivityInterpreters; WhatsApp calls; prototype printouts (D-7).
Confirming our own ideasPast experience first, prototypes only at the end; kill criteria set upfront.

Out of the sprint

  • A mass survey of 300+ stores — sprint 2, once interviews provide the answer options.
  • Hypotheses H4–H6 stay in the backlog.
  • Launching experiments, development, changing credit terms.

How planning decisions were made

  • 1I started from what Jabu already has, so the hypotheses improve existing features rather than invent new ones.
  • 2One sprint metric (M1 repeat rate) and two guardrails, so growth is not bought with bad debt or expensive delivery.
  • 3Success and kill criteria were written before the research, not after.
  • 4The 8 SP task was split: interviews and the concept test are separate tasks with different risks.
  • 5The researcher keeps a buffer: 21 SP of 24. The mass survey moved to sprint 2 — it doesn’t change this week’s decisions.
  • 6Every task is tied to a hypothesis, a day and dependencies — the critical path is visible.
  • 7Estimates explain uncertainty and dependencies, not just hours.
