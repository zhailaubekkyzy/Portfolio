Customer Discovery for Jabu · YC S21

Jabu is a B2B platform delivering groceries from manufacturers and large distributors to neighbourhood stores in Namibia, Zambia and South Africa. The task: set up a Customer Discovery process for the team — a task workflow in Jira, solution hypotheses and a research backlog, so every hypothesis travels from idea to a “validated / invalidated” decision.

Each task is one hypothesis or one study. The status colour comes from its Jira category: grey — To Do, blue — In Progress, green — Done. Labels on the arrows are transition names.

How to set it up in Jira: a company-managed project → Project settings → Workflows → each status has a category, each transition has a name and a description, global “from any status” transitions are off so the diagram reads as arrows.

Statuses

Nine statuses cover the full cycle, from a raw idea to a decision. On Hold exists because Discovery work often stalls for reasons outside the team — a respondent goes silent or there is no access to a distributor.

StatusCategoryMeaning
BacklogTo DoAn idea or hypothesis is logged but not yet scored.
Ready for ResearchTo DoScored with ICE and written in the template — ready to pick up.
On HoldTo DoWork paused for an external reason, noted in a comment.
Research PlanningIn ProgressChoosing the method, writing the interview script, setting the success criterion.
RecruitingIn ProgressFinding respondents: store owners, distributors, drivers.
InterviewsIn ProgressRunning interviews, field visits, prototype tests.
SynthesisIn ProgressClustering answers, checking against the criterion, drafting the conclusion.
ValidatedDoneCriterion met — the hypothesis goes to the product backlog.
InvalidatedDoneCriterion not met — we record what we learned.

Solution hypotheses

Format: “We believe [segment] [pain]. If we give them [solution], [metric] will change.” The criterion says upfront what counts as validation, so the “Validated / Invalidated” decision follows a rule, not a feeling.

H1Neighbourhood store ownersICE 448
We believe
they lose half a day and taxi money driving to a cash & carry for stock
Solution
order by 6 pm — delivered tomorrow, no minimum order
Metric
orders per week
Validated if
≥ 6 of 10 respondents restock in person more than once a week and name the trip as the top pain
How we test
10 interviews + field visits in Windhoek and Lusaka
H3Neighbourhood store ownersICE 441
We believe
ordering in WhatsApp is easier for them than installing a separate app
Solution
ordering via a WhatsApp bot with a catalogue
Metric
order completion rate
Validated if
in a prototype test ≥ 70% of stores complete an order in the bot unaided
How we test
Wizard-of-Oz test: a human takes WhatsApp orders
H4Neighbourhood store ownersICE 288
We believe
they find out an item is out of stock only when a customer asks for it
Solution
reorder reminders based on purchase history
Metric
repeat order rate
Validated if
≥ 6 of 10 describe a recent lost sale
How we test
last-time interviews (JTBD)
H5Stores in ZambiaICE 252
We believe
cash on delivery creates risk for drivers and delays money
Solution
mobile-money payment on delivery
Metric
share of cashless payments
Validated if
≥ 60% of stores already accept mobile money from their customers
How we test
interviews with stores and drivers
H2Neighbourhood store ownersICE 225
We believe
they lack working capital, so they buy little and often run out of fast-moving goods
Solution
7-day pay-later for proven stores
Metric
average order value
Validated if
≥ 5 of 10 would order more with pay-later and name an amount
How we test
interviews + order history analysis
H6Manufacturers and distributorsICE 160
We believe
they cannot see what sells where in small retail
Solution
a paid demand dashboard by area
Metric
willingness to pay, pilots
Validated if
≥ 2 of 6 suppliers agree to a paid pilot
How we test
B2B interviews + dashboard mock-up
