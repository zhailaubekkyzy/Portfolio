Product case · Retention & monetization

Retention FinanceApp

FinanceApp is a fintech app for budgeting, investing and loans, with 1.2M active users in 5 countries. Acquisition grows every quarter, while 6-month retention falls from 48% to 35%. I found the main lever — moving users into a second feature — and built a three-focus strategy.

A training case from a product management course: the data come from the brief, not a real product
6-month retention48% → 35%Q1 2022 vs Q1 2023 cohort
Retained from growth15.5%of 120K extra new users year over year
Accounting only60% · 25%share of the base and its retention: the largest and leakiest segment
Countries C, D, E≈ 50%of revenue from 35% of users

What I found

  1. 1Growth runs on volume, not quality. New users grew 67% in a year, retained users only 22%. Each new cohort retains worse than the one before.
  2. 2The main lever is a second feature, not a country. Investing lifts retention from 25% to 58%, all three features to 72%. Countries differ by only ±6% on LTV/CAC.
  3. 3Loans earn, but don’t retain. 32% vs 25% for the base: once the loan is repaid, there is no reason to come back.

What I do

  1. 0No more acquisition spend while retention falls. Otherwise we pay CAC for users who leave.
  2. F1Accounting only → investing. Moving 10% of the base adds about +3.3 pp of overall retention.
  3. F2Loans as a bridge. Loan → repayment budget → a “now save for yourself” offer at the last payment.
  4. F3C, D, E → Ultra. Half the revenue at the highest CAC: keep these users in the plan with 72% retention.

Assumptions and data checks

  1. 1ARPU is monthly, churn is per 6 months. So I show LTV twice: with the brief’s formula and adjusted ×6. The country ranking stays the same; the payback conclusion changes.
  2. 2Subscription prices don’t match ARPU. $1,500–3,000 vs ARPU of $4.5–13.5 — likely different units or currency. I leave them out of the calculations.
  3. 3Retention by feature is correlation, not cause. Loyal users may simply add more features. I test causality with an A/B test.
  4. 4Retention by country isn’t given. Country conclusions rest on unit economics, not retention. This is a limitation of the analysis.
  5. ✓The data are consistent. Weighted retention by feature is 37.7%, by cohort 41.6%: the same order of magnitude.

Task 5

Strategy: three focuses

Principle: stop the retention decline first, then grow acquisition. All three focuses move users into segments that stay.

F1

Accounting only → investing

Why
60% of the base at 25% retention. Investing lifts it to 58%. Moving 10% of the base ≈ +3.3 pp overall retention.
How
Customer interviews on barriers (fear, confusion, small budgets), then an A/B test of the offer: a first small investment straight from the budget.
Metric
Share of users with 2+ features; 6-month retention of the test group.
F2

Loans as a bridge, not a dead end

Why
Loans barely retain (32%) but bring users with strong intent. Today the last payment is the churn point.
How
Loan → a budget to repay calmly (also cuts delinquency risk) → at the last payment an offer: “now put this amount aside for yourself”.
Metric
Retention 3 months after loan payoff; share moving into investing.
F3

C, D, E → Ultra

Why
A third of users, half the revenue, the highest CAC: every loss hurts. Ultra retains 72%.
How
An Ultra trial for active two-feature users, local use cases, priority support.
Metric
Ultra share and LTV/CAC in C, D and E.

What I don’t do: reshuffle budget between countries (only ±6% difference) or launch new features while the current ones don’t retain.

How I validate

Before scaling

  1. 1Customer interviews. Not “do you like investing” but “what stops you from starting”. The answer shapes the offer.
  2. 2A/B test. Randomly split accounting-only users with similar tenure: one group gets the investing offer, the other doesn’t. Randomization removes the “loyal users add features anyway” effect.
  3. 3Early metric. Six months is too long to wait: I track day-30 activity, first checking on past data that it predicts 6-month retention.
  4. 4Guardrails. Loan delinquencies, notification opt-outs, support tickets.
